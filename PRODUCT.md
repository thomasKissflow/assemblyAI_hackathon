# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Home cooks making a meal with several dishes that must reach the table hot at the same moment. That includes people hosting dinner, busy parents cooking a weeknight meal, beginner cooks, and meal-kit customers. They're standing in a home kitchen with a laptop or tablet propped on the counter one to two metres away. Their hands are in dough, oil or raw chicken, and their eyes are on the stove. They glance at the screen between stirs; they never touch it mid-cook.

## Product Purpose

Heard, Chef runs the timing of a multi-dish dinner by voice. It plans every dish backwards from serving time, calls each step out loud when it's due, and re-plans everything the moment the cook reports a problem ("the curry needs ten more minutes", "guests are running late"). Success is dinner served together, hot and on time, without the cook ever washing their hands to touch a screen or doing timing arithmetic in their head.

## Positioning

The dinner timer you can talk back to: tell it what the food is doing, and it re-plans every dish and calls the next move before you have to ask.

## Brand Personality

A calm head chef on the pass. Short, confident kitchen calls ("Rice on now." "Hold the naan." "Heard."), never chatty and never flustered, steady when the cook is not. Three words: calm, decisive, kitchen-true.

## Anti-references

- Workflow or project-management software: Gantt bars, resource lanes, admin dashboards. Thomas explicitly wants this far from Kissflow territory.
- A generic smart-speaker assistant: a glowing blue orb that sets one timer at a time.

## Design Principles

- **Glanceable from across the kitchen.** At any moment one thing matters most, the next call, and it's readable from two metres.
- **The voice leads, the screen confirms.** Every call is spoken and mirrored on screen at the same moment. The cook can run the whole dinner by ear.
- **Code does the maths, Chef does the talking.** Every time shown or spoken comes from the planner, never from the model's guess.
- **Calm under pressure.** When plans slip, the interface re-settles smoothly and says only what changed.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific requirements were given. Working defaults: WCAG AA contrast or better (glanceability pushes most text well past it), a reduced-motion alternative for every animation, and every voice action reachable by keyboard or mouse too (mute, pause, skip).
